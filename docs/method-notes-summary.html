Massing Studio · In brief

Method notes in brief

The short version: what the tool computes, and — the part that matters most when you cite it — what it does not model at all. It draws a plan, extrudes it by a height, and exports the result. There is no analysis of any kind in it, and no solver anywhere in the source. The full method notes give the source values and the references behind every statement here.

Audience Architecture students and staff Method Plan drawing · straight extrusion · measurement · no analysis Runs Entirely in the browser Version 1.1.0 · 26 September 2026

Three things to know before you cite a number

  1. "GFA" here is a sum of footprints, not of floors. The status bar's GFA, and each level's area in the Levels panel, add up every extruded shape's plan area once, whatever its height. A 9 m volume drawn as one shape counts once, not three storeys, and overlapping shapes are both counted in full. It is a true floor area only when each storey is drawn as its own non-overlapping shape — and nothing checks that it is.
  2. Snapping is stronger than the Snap checkbox, and weaker than the grid. Snapping to an existing corner fires whether or not Snap is on — drawing, moving, scaling, editing points, placing dimensions. With Snap off everything else goes free-hand, with nothing marking a coordinate as off-grid. And the 3D push/pull rounds a height to 0.1 m, not to the grid, unless it lands near another volume's top or a level.
  3. Nothing here is analysis. No module computes a structural, thermal, daylight or cost quantity of any kind, and there is no solver — iterative or otherwise — anywhere in the source.

What it computes

Geometry only: snapped coordinates, distances (in plan and in 3D), footprint area and perimeter, volume as area × height, a smooth curve through the points you click, and the footprints of Intersect, Union and Subtract. Height is still the only thing that becomes a 3D dimension — every volume is a footprint carried straight up.

The one formula worth naming is the shoelace formula for polygon area, named as such in the source's own comment. Since version 1.1.0 its result is shown to you, holes subtracted — with trap 1's warning about what the totals add up.

Not modelled at all

This list is still long, and that is the honest shape of a massing tool rather than a gap to apologise for.

Out
Any quantity beyond geometry
No structural, thermal, daylight or cost quantity is computed anywhere.
Out
Solid modelling beyond a straight extrusion
No sloped or tapered surfaces, no roofs, no openings in a wall. Booleans work only between straight vertical extrusions, and a courtyard is the only kind of hole.
Out
Material, thickness, or relationships
A shape has a colour, an opacity and a height, and nothing else. A boolean result forgets its inputs; a copy stacked on a volume doesn't follow it.
Partly
Storeys
Levels stack floors at set elevations, with true orthographic elevations, but a level is an elevation with a name: nothing checks overlaps, or that a volume fits within its level.

How exact the numbers are

The arithmetic is exact for straight edges — no solver, nothing converging. The honest questions are about resolution and representation.

1 grid unit
What the grid costs
With Snap on, a point drawn, moved, scaled or edited lands on a whole grid unit — one metre by default. Heights snap to 0.1 m, or to another volume's top or a level.
32 sides
Circles and curves
A circle's displayed area is the exact πr², but its 3D volume, exports and boolean results use a 32-sided polygon — about 0.64 % less area. A curve is 12 straight segments per span everywhere.
full double
Precision in the exports
DXF and OBJ write coordinates at full precision with no rounding. STL is the exception: binary STL stores every vertex as a 32-bit float.
Not guarded
Where a wall push/pull can go wrong
A drag that would shrink the area below five per cent is rejected, but area alone cannot catch every way a polygon folds in on itself, and near-parallel neighbouring edges fall back to a straight-line approximation.
None
What is actually asserted
There is no automated test suite anywhere in this application's source. What the tool computes has been read from the code; it has not been checked against itself by any suite of its own.

What survives an export

Both ways
Save, then open
As .json, or with Save as .obj — ordinary OBJ geometry for other programs, with the whole project carried in comment lines they ignore. Either reopens exactly.
Footprints
DXF export, then DXF import
One polyline per shape, or a circle; they come back editable, but on one level, at no height, with curves as polygons and courtyards as separate shapes.
Extrusions
Opening another program's .obj
Straight vertical extrusions are rebuilt as editable shapes, one level per floor height; anything else — a roof, a dome — comes in as a static reference.
One way
STL, and image export
STL is geometry for printing; PNG/JPEG export, at up to four times the view or a custom width, on a transparent or white background, is a picture.

Against SketchUp, BIM and CAD

Agrees on push/pull — dragging a top face or a side wall is the same operation SketchUp performs — on Intersect, Union and Subtract between solids, on stacked levels read in orthographic elevation, and on DXF as a real interchange format.

Diverges in having no object model beyond a footprint and a height. A Revit wall carries a type, a thickness, layers and relationships; a Massing Studio “wall” is one side of an extruded footprint, and a level knows nothing of the storey it names. No BIM metadata, no analysis, no history — move one shape and nothing else reacts.

Where it stops

The tool is built to teach the relationship between a plan and a volume — a fast way to test a footprint and a few storeys against each other before opening something heavier. Move to SketchUp, Rhino or a BIM package the moment you need materials, assemblies, or an answer that has to be checked rather than sketched. It contains no analysis of any kind, deliberately, so that what it does do stays legible — and it has no test suite of its own, so nothing in it has been checked against itself.
